# Risk and Governance

## In one sentence

AI governance establishes the decision rights, policies, responsibilities, evidence, and controls through which an institution pursues AI opportunities within its legal and risk boundaries.

## Executive mental model

NIST organizes AI risk management into four mutually reinforcing functions:

```text
GOVERN - establish accountability, policy, culture, and inventory
MAP    - understand context, purpose, people, benefits, and harms
MEASURE- assess performance, impact, and uncertainty
MANAGE - prioritize and treat risk; monitor and respond
```

Governance should be proportionate. A writing assistant and a system influencing employment decisions should not pass through identical review.

## Governance mechanisms

- AI system inventory and risk classification;
- named business, technical, data, and risk owners;
- acceptable-use and prohibited-use rules;
- impact, privacy, security, and legal assessments;
- stage gates and independent challenge;
- documentation and decision logs;
- incident, appeal, and redress processes;
- supplier and model oversight; and
- monitoring, audit, change control, and retirement.

## Why executives should care

Governance is not a committee that receives a finished project. It is how an organization makes better decisions early, enables lower-risk innovation quickly, and retains accountability throughout the lifecycle.

## Questions to ask

- Is the use prohibited, regulated, or high impact?
- Who accepts residual risk and owns the outcome?
- Are affected people represented and given appropriate recourse?
- Can we demonstrate what was decided and why?
- Is governance integrated into delivery and portfolio review?
