It improves the four-class narrative
I would now frame the whole course around four increasingly sophisticated questions.

## Class 1 — UNDERSTAND
What is AI, what changed with GenAI, and what are its limitations?

GenAI is powerful but imperfect.

## Class 2 — IMAGINE
What can we do with it—and how do we separate capability from hype?

Prototyping is cheap. Reliable business systems are not.

Students learn modalities, capabilities, opportunity discovery, demo vs prototype vs production, and how to critically evaluate “AI magic.”

## Class 3 — DESIGN
What do we need around the model to make it useful and reliable?

AI value comes from the system, not just the model.

Context engineering, RAG, memory, workflows, agents, tools, harnesses, evals, guardrails, observability, human-in-the-loop.

## Class 4 — LEAD
How do we decide what to invest in and manage AI in the organization?

Move from technological possibility to measurable organizational value.

Strategy, prioritization, build/buy, governance, risk, ROI, organizational change, pilot → production → scale.

There is also a useful phrase you could repeat throughout the four classes:

“Possible is not the same as reliable. Reliable is not the same as valuable.”

That captures a large part of the executive intuition you're trying to develop.
