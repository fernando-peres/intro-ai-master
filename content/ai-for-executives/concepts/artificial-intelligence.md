# Artificial Intelligence

## In one sentence

Artificial intelligence is the field of creating machine-based systems that infer from inputs to produce outputs such as predictions, content, recommendations, or decisions that can affect physical or virtual environments.

## Executive mental model

```text
Environment or business need
        -> inputs and data
        -> AI system
        -> prediction / content / recommendation / decision
        -> human or machine action
        -> outcome and feedback
```

AI is an umbrella term. Machine learning, deep learning, generative AI, computer vision, optimization, and some rule-based systems can all sit beneath it.

## What it is not

- AI is not a single technology or product.
- AI is not necessarily autonomous.
- AI is not synonymous with ChatGPT or large language models.
- AI does not remove institutional accountability for the outcome.

## Why executives should care

AI changes the cost and speed of prediction, interpretation, content creation, and some forms of decision support. Its effect depends on how those capabilities are inserted into a workflow, who uses them, and what action follows.

The useful executive question is not "Do we need AI?" It is "Which outcome or workflow needs to improve, and is an AI capability an appropriate part of the response?"

## Questions to ask

- What specific output does the system produce?
- Who uses or is affected by that output?
- What action or decision follows?
- Why is AI better than a policy change, simple rule, or conventional software?
- Who remains accountable when the output is wrong?

## Example

A bank may use several forms of AI: fraud prediction, document extraction, a knowledge assistant, and personalized recommendations. These are different systems with different data, controls, and risks, even though all are called AI.
