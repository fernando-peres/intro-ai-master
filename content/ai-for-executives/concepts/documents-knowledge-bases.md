# Documents and Knowledge Bases

## In one sentence

Documents contain institutional information; a knowledge base organizes, governs, and makes selected information usable for people and systems.

## Executive mental model

```text
Files are not yet a trustworthy knowledge system.

Documents
  -> ownership and permissions
  -> structure and metadata
  -> quality and version control
  -> indexing and retrieval
  -> approved knowledge available in a workflow
```

A shared drive with thousands of files may contain knowledge, but it can also contain duplicates, obsolete policies, contradictory versions, inaccessible scans, and information users are not authorized to see.

## What must be governed

- authoritative source and owner;
- publication and approval status;
- version and effective date;
- access permissions;
- retention and deletion;
- language, format, and accessibility;
- metadata and relationships; and
- update and review cadence.

## Why executives should care

An AI knowledge assistant exposes the quality of institutional knowledge. Many apparent model failures are actually source, permission, retrieval, or workflow failures. The project may therefore require information-management work before AI work.

## Questions to ask

- Which source is authoritative when documents disagree?
- Who owns freshness and correction?
- Can access controls be preserved through ingestion and retrieval?
- How will citations let users verify an answer?
- What happens when a source is amended or deleted?

## Example

An HR assistant should retrieve only approved, current policy passages applicable to the employee's jurisdiction and role, cite them, and route unusual cases to HR.
