## General Objectives

This course unit provides a practical introduction to Artificial Intelligence and Machine Learning, oriented towards their use in business. It develops an accessible understanding of the main AI approaches, including traditional machine learning, generative AI and agentic AI systems, without requiring mathematical depth or programming skills.

The course explores use cases across organisational functions and prepares students to identify opportunities, translate business problems into AI use cases, understand the data and resources required, assess value, feasibility, limitations and risks, and structure an implementation proposal. Learning is based on demonstrations, case studies, low-code and no-code tools, applied exercises and the development of a business project, in which students are expected to use generative AI critically and transparently.

## Sustainable Development Goals (SDG)

The course unit contributes in particular to the following Sustainable Development Goals:

- **SDG 8 - Decent Work and Economic Growth:** by analysing the potential of AI to increase productivity and transform organisational processes, and its implications for work.
- **SDG 9 - Industry, Innovation and Infrastructure:** by exploring AI-enabled digital solutions and innovation.
- **SDG 12 - Responsible Consumption and Production:** by considering the costs, energy consumption and environmental footprint of AI solutions when assessing their value and feasibility.

## Enrolment Requirements

Not applicable. No prior knowledge of programming or machine learning model development is required.

## Learning Outcomes

Knowledge, skills, and competences to be developed by the students, and their compatibility with the teaching modality:

1. **LO1.** Explain the fundamental concepts of Artificial Intelligence, Machine Learning, Deep Learning and Generative AI, distinguishing them from traditional programming.
2. **LO2.** Distinguish predictive, generative and agentic systems and identify their business application contexts.
3. **LO3.** Understand, at a conceptual level, how models learn from data, produce results and are evaluated.
4. **LO4.** Identify opportunities to apply AI across functional areas and industries.
5. **LO5.** Translate a business problem into an AI use case and select the most suitable technological approach.
6. **LO6.** Interpret the results, metrics, errors, limitations and risks of an AI solution, including outputs produced by generative AI.
7. **LO7.** Understand how agentic AI systems work and their potential to automate and transform organisational processes.
8. **LO8.** Make a preliminary assessment of the value, feasibility, data, integrations, resources and risks involved in implementing an AI solution.
9. **LO9.** Design, prototype and communicate an AI solution proposal for a business challenge to technical and non-technical audiences.

## Programmatic Contents

1. **AI in the business context:** Recent evolution of AI; traditional programming, AI, machine learning, deep learning and generative AI; capabilities, limitations, trends and value creation.
2. **How machine learning works:** Data, variables, training and prediction; supervised and unsupervised learning; classification, regression, segmentation and recommendation; a conceptual view of decision trees, ensembles and neural networks.
3. **Model evaluation and interpretation:** Training, validation and test; generalisation and overfitting; performance metrics; false positives and false negatives; explainability, bias and monitoring.
4. **How generative AI works:** Foundation models and large language models; tokenisation, embeddings, attention and probabilistic generation; training, fine-tuning and inference; context, temperature and other settings; technical limitations, hallucinations and output evaluation.
5. **Generative AI in organisations:** Prompting and interaction design; content generation and transformation; copilots and conversational assistants; retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) and the use of organisational knowledge; applications across business functions; quality, cost, confidentiality, intellectual property and human validation of outputs.
6. **Agentic AI and intelligent automation:** From assistants to agents; goals, planning, memory, tools and actions; agentic workflows; multi-agent systems; integration with processes and systems; human oversight, security, control and conditions for deployment in organisations.
7. **From opportunity to use case:** Identifying and prioritising opportunities; problem framing; selecting the technological approach; data, integrations, value, feasibility, risk and success indicators (KPIs); costs, energy consumption and environmental footprint; build, buy or partner decisions; pilot and implementation planning.

- Contents 1 and 2 support LO1 to LO3, establishing the common language and conceptual understanding needed to interpret different AI approaches.
- Contents 2 and 3 support LO3 and LO6, explaining how models work, how their performance is assessed and the consequences of errors in different business contexts.
- Contents 4 and 5 support LO1 to LO4 and LO6, providing an accessible understanding of how generative AI works and how it is used in organisations. The small use case built in content 5 also supports LO9.
- Content 6 supports LO2, LO4 and LO7, exploring agentic systems, their components, applications and conditions for deployment in organisational processes.
- Content 7 supports LO4, LO5, LO8 and LO9, guiding students in identifying, prioritising, structuring and communicating AI use cases with implementation potential. ## Teaching Methodologies

The course unit follows an active, practice-oriented learning model suited to an Executive Master. Sessions start from business problems and cases, introduce the concepts needed to analyse them and end with application, experimentation and reflection activities.

- **Short, focused lectures:** to introduce essential concepts, a common language and decision frameworks.
- **Case studies and business examples:** to relate AI technologies to organisational processes, functions and results.
- **Demonstrations and hands-on workshops:** using accessible low-code or no-code tools to experiment with predictive models, generative AI and agents.
- **Guest session:** dedicated to agentic AI in organisations, including real cases, a demonstration and discussion of deployment conditions.
- **Collaborative, challenge-based learning:** groups develop an AI use case and solution proposal throughout the course, starting from the project brief in class 1 and receiving formative feedback on a use-case canvas after class 3.
- **Critical use of generative AI:** students use generative AI tools throughout the course and are asked to verify, critique and document their outputs, putting into practice the human validation covered in content 5.
- **Executive presentation and discussion:** to develop the ability to communicate value, requirements, limitations and risks to different stakeholders.

## Assessment

| Assessment element | Weight | Type |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Applied AI project: AI business case and prototype or demonstration, with an AI-use annex | 45% | Group, with individual adjustment by peer assessment |
| Executive presentation and defence of the project | 25% | Group presentation; Q&A graded individually |
| Individual quiz, in person | 30% | Individual |

The applied project must include: business context and problem; users and current process; value creation opportunity; proposed solution; data, tools and integrations; prototype or conceptual workflow; risks and limitations; success indicators; pilot and implementation plan; and an annex on the use of generative AI.

**Presentation and defence:** each group presents its project in the assessment session held after class 7. During the questions, any member may be asked about any part of the project, and each member's answers are graded individually.

**Individual quiz:** in the same session, each student answers a short quiz (approximately 30 minutes) based on brief business scenarios rather than recall: for example, identifying the AI family behind a described case, choosing an evaluation metric given the cost of errors, spotting the limitations of an AI-generated output, or identifying the components and risks of an agentic workflow. The quiz is taken in person, without AI tools.

**Peer assessment:** individual project grades may be adjusted, based on a confidential peer assessment, to reflect each member's contribution.

**Use of generative AI:** generative AI tools may be used, and are encouraged, in the applied project and its presentation, provided their use is declared in an annex identifying the tools, their purpose and how outputs were verified or corrected. Students are responsible for the accuracy of all submitted content. Confidential or personal data from partner organisations must not be entered into tools that do not guarantee its protection. The individual quiz is taken without AI tools. These rules apply in addition to the NOVA IMS Rules of Conduct and Code of Honour.

**Second assessment period:** an individual in-person quiz (50%) and an individual AI use-case proposal, defended in an oral examination (50%).

## Evidence That the Teaching and Assessment Methodologies Are Appropriate for the Learning Outcomes

- Short lectures, cases and demonstrations support LO1 to LO4, providing conceptual understanding and contact with real applications.
- Hands-on workshops allow students to experiment with solutions and interpret results, supporting LO3, LO6 and LO7, and prepare students for the scenario-based questions of the individual quiz.
- The guest session reinforces LO7 and brings current market practice and real conditions for adopting agentic AI into the course.
- The individual quiz assesses, individually and in person, each student's command of the core concepts applied to business scenarios: identifying the AI family and approach, understanding how models learn and are evaluated, recognising the limitations of generative AI outputs, and identifying the components and risks of agentic systems (LO1 to LO4, LO6 and LO7).
- The applied project and its defence jointly assess LO4, LO5, LO8 and LO9, and LO7 where the solution involves agentic components, requiring the design and communication of a viable, organisation-oriented proposal. The individually graded questions verify each member's understanding of the whole project.
- The AI-use annex in the project assesses the critical and verified use of generative AI that the course teaches, and the quiz includes questions on the limitations of AI-generated outputs (LO6).
